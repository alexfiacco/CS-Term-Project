Brand chips v1: seven chip looks become three

Today a chip can be any of seven things: a square lane tag, a round mood pill, Bodoni in a pill, a blue "understood as" token, a Sort or Filters pill, a "›" link chip or a "#" hash chip, plus a dashed "+ 12 more". The proposal keeps three, as in brand-pass-v1 fix 4. The filter chip opens or removes something (▾, ×, a count). The choice chip toggles a word, such as a mood, an answer or a suggestion. The lane goes somewhere: it is an underlined Bodoni italic title with ›, not a box. The filter and choice chips share one shape, the 32 capsule in UI 13 with a --line-2 edge. Only their "on" state is still open, and that is what options A to C test. The lane is the same in all three. Each row puts the owner's real screenshot next to the same content rebuilt.

Decide

  1. A, the quiet dot, for "on". The chosen chip gets a hairline cream outline, semibold words and a cream dot. Blue is left for data and you, and cream is left for the one main action on the screen (Tonight's "Just pick for me" ticket, Action!). It pairs with switch-v2 A or B. If you pick switch C, use the dot on the switch and give chips option B instead, so a one-of switch and a many-of chip never look the same.
  2. B, cream paper, if the chosen state must be seen at a glance across a whole drawer (Filters, Pitch). It is the strongest, but four cream chips plus the cream CTA in one drawer is a lot of cream.
  3. C, keep today's blue tint (rule 1172) and only fold the shapes. This is the cheapest. It keeps blue meaning "chosen" as well as data.
  4. Lanes become titles, not boxes, everywhere (Your lanes, the film page's lane, popular themes in search): .lane in Bodoni italic, a hairline underline and ›. This retires the square tag, the › link chip and the "#" chip.
  5. Retire the dashed "+ 12 more" for a plain "12 more ›" text link, and retire Bodoni-in-a-capsule. The writers' room suggestions become choice chips, and Bodoni stays in titles and lanes.
  6. The Pitch ↑ ↓ controls live inside the chip, after a hairline, so a mood is one chip, not a chip plus two loose arrows.
