Search for what you know, Pitch for what you don't

The owner: "Should we keep Describe and regular search separate, or put them together?" Today they are half merged: the field says "Describe a film, or a title", its open state leads with three italic "Describe it" sentences, and a sentence typed there can turn into a Pitch. Here they get one job each. The search field finds things you can name: a film, a person. The Pitch button (and its drawer, see pitch-drawer-v1) is for a film you can only describe. When someone types a sentence into search anyway, one row offers to Pitch it instead, and nothing is spent until they say so.

Recommended: two jobs, one bridge

Alternative · one merged box

No Pitch button, Pitch is a row

One field for everything. The Pitch button goes, and a sentence turns into a Pitch row at the top of the results.

Open. The placeholder has to explain two jobs, and with no button nothing on screen says Pitch exists until you type a sentence.

A sentence. Works, and it is tidy. But Pitch, the feature with its own name and a monthly allowance, is reduced to a search result most people never trigger.

Recommendation: keep them separate, with one bridge

Two different questions. Search answers "where is the thing I can name", and wants to be instant, free and literal. Pitch answers "what should I watch that is like this", is slower, ranked by your match, and counts against free pitches. One box that tries to guess which you meant gets both a little wrong: today a title can be mistaken for a description, and the field's open state is half filled with prompts for the other job.

The button keeps Pitch discoverable. It is pinned beside the field with its own mark, and opens the drawer. The merged box hides Pitch behind typing a sentence, which most people will not try in a search field.

The bridge catches the rest. When a sentence lands in search, the top row offers Pitch, and the drawer opens with the sentence already in it. Nobody has to retype, and nobody spends a pitch by accident.

Build notes

iPhone. In BrowseScreen, the placeholder becomes "Search films or people" whether or not Pitch is on; remove describeIt from the empty state. recutRow becomes "Pitch this instead ›" with the sentence under it, and opens the Pitch drawer with the sentence as a prefill (RecutLink(prefill:), not sentence:, so it does not run). onSubmit stops calling openRecut: Return searches.

The sentence test. Widen RecutEntry.looksLikeDescription: true at four or more words, or any of like, but, something, anything, for when, feels, meets; still false when the query is exactly a film's title.

Web. The same in GlobalSearch.tsx and BrowseHub.tsx (placeholder), the Describe it block in Recut.tsx goes, and its search row opens RecutSheet on its ask with the sentence filled in.
