For You and Best for you are not tag states. "For You" is the first tab's name: every card in it is for you, so a "For you" line on each card would repeat the tab. "Best for you" is a sort, not a card: Browse's "Every film, best for you first" and a profile's Films sort. A friend's pick shows as the people line under the title (@sam 4.0★, rule 1074), never on the poster; a new release's "In theaters" sits in the meta line. So the tag has exactly four words today: Last night, Today, This week, Starter.
Plain cards: no dateline (recommended). The line means "this card has a day or a place in a run". If every card carried one ("For you", "Picked for you"), it would stop meaning anything and the feed gets the text back that rule 1114 took away. With D3 the 22pt gap is kept on every For You card, empty on plain ones, so the poster is one size all the way down and nothing jumps as you swipe from This week 4 of 4 into the feed.

Recommended: D3, the compact dateline

  • It is the D the owner liked, small caps between hairlines over the poster, made compact: 10pt caps in the gap under the tab strip, its hairlines only as wide as the poster, so it reads as the poster's own headline and not a second rule under the tabs.
  • It costs 6pt, not 26. The gap above the poster goes from 16 to 22; the poster loses 6pt of height (4pt of width), which nobody will see. D1 takes 26pt off every tagged poster and D2 22pt.
  • Nothing jumps. The 22pt gap is kept on every For You card, empty on plain ones, so posters are one size the whole way down.
  • Copy: TODAY · FRI, NOV 6, THIS WEEK · 2 OF 4, LAST NIGHT · THU, NOV 5, STARTER · 1 OF 6. The word in ink, the rest grey. The dots go: "2 of 4" says the run in the dateline's own voice.
  • Off the poster, so it never fights the art, Trailer or share, on a white poster or a black one; no red of its own.

D1, the v1 dateline

  • The fullest newspaper look, hairlines to the gutters; the cost is 26pt of poster on every tagged card, and a 26pt jump into plain cards unless the gap is kept on all of them.

D2, the left kicker

  • Lines up with the title's left edge, so it reads as an eyebrow again, the thing rule 1114 removed; "Today’s pick" and "Last night’s film" are longer words for the same news.
