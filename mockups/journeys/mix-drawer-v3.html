Each type in How you got shows its three films and "172 of your favorites ›". The owner asked for an average rating beside the count. It can be computed: every type already stores its films (archetype.evidence[type].slugs) and your library has your rating for each. Drawn with @alexfiacco's real read and real averages, computed read-only from Supabase. They come out almost identical (4.2★, 4.2★, 4.2★), and that matters for whether to ship it: see the notes. Recommendation: the list header, not the rows

Every type comes out at 4.2★, and for most people it will. The films in a type are your favorites: films rated above your own average (3.63). So every one is 4★ or more and the averages pile up just above it: 4.22, 4.18, 4.19. Three identical numbers in three rows say nothing about why you're a Nihilist, and they make the evidence look templated.

So I'd leave the drawer rows as they are and put the average in the list header only, where it sits over the stamps and can be checked: 172 of your favorites · avg 4.2★. If the owner wants the rows to carry it anyway, it fits as drawn (the second frame).

A figure that does differ: how many you rated 5★ (15 / 12 / 8), but it tracks the count. The figure that would really answer "how much do I like this kind of film" is your average over every film of the type, not just favorites. That needs the model to place your other rated films too (it only places favorites today).

What it needs

No new server field for the list header. /api/archetype-films already returns each film's rating; the drawer averages them (films.filter(r).avg) once they load and adds · avg 4.2★ to the sub. The sub shows the count alone until the films arrive.

For the drawer rows, a server field. The rows draw from the stored read, which has slugs but no ratings, and the rows can't wait on one fetch per type. Add avg to ArchetypeEvidence in archetypeEvidence() (apply.ts, where the read already has your ratings loaded), pass it through profile-display.ts into evidence.line, and let the archetype-evidence backfill fill older reads. Visitors: avg 4.2★ is their rating, so it's hidden with Share my activity off, as the evidence already is.

Format: one decimal, Newsreader lining figures, the one star, blue: the stamp's 4.5★.
