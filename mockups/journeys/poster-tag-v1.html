Poster tag v1: six ways to say Today and This week

The owner: "I don't like how the tags like Today or This week look, we need to redesign those." Today's tag (rule 1114) is a dark-grey glass pill at the poster's top left with a short red dash, then the word, and for This week four dots. It reads as a third grey control beside the Trailer pill and the share circle, and its red dash is a second red on a screen whose red is already the oxblood poster shadow. Six options in Montage's own language (ticket, slate, marquee, dateline, a Bodoni leaf, a sprocket row), each drawn as Today on a white poster (Farewell My Concubine), This week 1 of 4 on a busy photo poster, and a corner check on white, photo and near-black posters. None adds red: the oxblood shadow stays the screen's one red. The same words carry over to the tag's other two uses: Last night and a newcomer's Starter · 1 of 4.

This week on the web
The stub reads THIS WEEK ¦ ●○○○ at 11.5px, 26 tall, on the poster's top edge. Same component on both platforms: iOS PosterTagView and the web's .pk-tag become one cream stub; the word list and the dots are unchanged.

Recommended: A, the ticket stub

  • It rhymes with the card. Under the poster is already a cream ticket stub (Seen it, Watchlist, Not for me) with notched ends; the tag becomes its small sibling, so the screen has two cream tickets and no grey pill fighting Trailer.
  • Always legible. It sits on the poster's top edge, half over the dark page, with a drop shadow; on a white poster the ink letters and the shadow carry it, on a black one the cream does.
  • Nothing at the bottom moves. Trailer and share keep their corners; the stub never meets them.
  • No red: the dash goes, the oxblood shadow is the one red. The date ("Fri · Nov 6") is new information, not decoration; This week's dots go on the stub's torn half.

Runner-up: D, the dateline

  • Off the poster, so never fights the art, Trailer or share, and reads like a newspaper's dateline. Costs 26pt of height on every card and pushes the stub buttons down.
  • Pick D if the owner wants posters left untouched entirely.

Not recommended

  • B slate: charming but busy; the stripes are a second pattern on the poster. C marquee: covers the top 30pt of every poster's art. E leaf: squeezes the title. F sprocket row: one more line of text, the "too much text" the owner removed in picks-tag-v1.
