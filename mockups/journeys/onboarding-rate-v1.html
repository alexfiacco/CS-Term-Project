Rate films you love, and the deck: three directions each

The owner on the first run: "the first 'rate films' and then you scroll through the catalog, I feel like that looks like any other app." The review behind it (onboarding-moments-v1 and v2): the first two minutes give back nothing about the person. Below, each screen as it is on origin/main today (web LovedStart.tsx and TasteTest.tsx, iPhone LovedStartScreen.swift, TasteDeckScreen.swift, PosterWall.swift, DNATease.swift), then three options with their own character. Rule 853 holds in every option: no match %, no archetype name and no taste read before 15 ratings. Every phone is 390pt; the web matches at 390 and centres the same column on a desktop. One red per screen, counted under each phone.

1 · Rate films you love

Today it is rows of popular films with chips and search, counting toward the deck's 30. It works, but it is a catalog: nothing on the screen is yours except a small "5★" under a poster. Each option gives your first 3 to 6 favourites a place that is clearly yours, and keeps the same count, the same rating drawer and the same hand-off to the deck.

A · The lobby and your shelf

The lobby
  • The rows become a lit poster case, three across, each with one plain line: year, genre, and "loved by many" when the crowd is 4★ and up with a big audience (the existing isConsensus test).
  • Every rating drops the poster onto Your shelf, six slots docked at the foot. The newest takes the oxblood offset, the one red. Picked posters dim in the case with their stars.
  • "Keep going" from the third. Past six the shelf scrolls and the count carries on to the deck's 30.
B · Admit one
Red on screen: 1, the newest stub
Admit one
  • Results read like a box office: poster, title, one fact line, and the stars you gave on the right.
  • Each rating prints a cream stub into a rail at the foot: "Admit one", the title, "5★ · 1995", a serial number down the tear. The house's ticket language (Tonight, movie night), so it feels like Montage.
  • The empty stub says "Admit one more" until there are six.
C · Your season
Red on screen: 1, the programme's offset
Your season
  • A repertory programme sits on top and typesets itself as you rate: number, title, year, your stars, with dotted lines waiting for the rest.
  • Under it, search and the same popular rows, smaller. The programme is the thing you are making; the catalog is where you find it.
  • The most "yours" of the three, but the rows lose height, so finding films is slower.

B · The reel
Red on screen: 1, the poster's offset
The reel
  • The bar becomes a cream film strip of 15 frames, the streak strip and the reel loader's look. Each film you rate is printed into the next frame as its poster; skips and Haven't seen it leave no frame. The next frame waits in blue.
  • It ends at the Film DNA tease, so the reel visibly feeds the read. At 15 it rolls on to a second strip toward 30.
  • "9 rated · 6 more for your read" under it. Progress only, fully inside rule 853.

Noted
  • Now and then (at most one every three ratings, never at 8, which is the halfway beat) a short line about what you just did slides under the bar for 3 seconds.
  • Inside rule 853, about choices only: "Four 5★ already. You know what you love." "You've seen 9 of 11." "Nothing under 3★ yet. Generous." "Half stars on three. Precise, noted."
  • Genre lines ("Not a horror person, noted.", "You like them slow.") are leans, which rule 853 keeps out; they wait for the owner.
