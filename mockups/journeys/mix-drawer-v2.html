How you got Nihilist, with the evidence

Each type in the drawer says what kind of film it is and how you sit against most people, but never how much of you it is. Proposed: one count line per row, from the same grouping that already picks your defining films. A gives the count and the bar's share; B gives the count beside the three films that count most. Drawn with @alexfiacco's stored read; every count is a stand-in (marked on the first row), as nothing stores them yet.

What exists for this today (result_json.archetype and the model)

FigureStored?Where, and what it really is
Share per type (17% / 8% / 12%)Storedarchetype.top[].weight and archetype.scores[].share. A weighted share, not a film count: each favorite counts by how far above your own average you rated it, each watch without a rating as a fraction of an average favorite (watchWeights, "ratio" mode), and each film spreads over several types by soft membership (sharesOf). The bar renormalises the three shown (mixPercents), so Nihilist is 17% of you but 46% of the bar.
Against most people (lift)Storedarchetype.scores[].lift (1.41 / 0.92 / 0.73, your share over the average member's, capped at 2), already shown as the vs line in three buckets (howYouGotRow).
Films behind each typeTwo titles, in a sentencearchetype.whySpecific[id] carries groupFilms' top two as text ("led by Se7en and Pulp Fiction"). No slugs, no posters, no third film. signatures has five posters but not which type each stands for.
Favorites, totalDerivableRated above your own average: 474 of 1,079 for @alexfiacco (mean 3.63, from library_json.rated). Not stored; the model computes it every read.
Favorites per type (the count)Computed, thrown awayreadPerson's home map (model.ts) gives every rated favorite to the shown type it belongs to most (15% or more). leadFilms is the lead type's whole list, so its length is the Nihilist count; the others are never kept. Unrated watches are left out of home on purpose.
"You rate these 0.6★ above your average"Not worth computingEvery film in home is already above your average by definition, so this line is always positive and says nothing about the type. Against Letterboxd would say more, but that is Film DNA's job, and the vs line already covers "more than most".

What would need computing

  • model.ts readPerson: return groupStats: { [label]: { count, top: slug[3] } } from home (count of rated films per shown type, and the first three by pull). Free: home and pull are already built for the five films.
  • apply.ts unifiedIdentity: store it on the archetype (say archetype.evidence[id] = { count, films: [{ slug, title, posterUrl }] }, posters from the facts it already loads) and the favorites total. Then profile-display.ts howYouGotRow passes it to both drawers.
  • Backfill: like backfillWhySpecific (identity-refresh.ts, capped 10 a run), because the films behind a type cannot be derived on read. Until a profile has it, the row draws as Now.
  • The bar should then use the groups' shares (r.groups[].share, the whole family a type stands for: Nihilist's group holds the Hardboiled and Midnight Cultist films too). Today it uses each type's own share, so a group's count could rank above a wider segment. Counts and the bar must agree, or the evidence argues with the picture.

Recommendation

  • B. A count alone is a stat; the count beside three of your own posters is the answer to "why Lovestruck?": these films, this many like them. It also retires the one-film "Se7en is one of yours." for something you can tap.
  • Count, no percentage. A's "46% of your mix" restates the bar, and next to 17% in any future copy it reads as a contradiction (46% is of the three shown, 17% of you). Rule 704 says no percentage is shown in How you got; B keeps that and adds a number people can check against their own diary.
  • Say "favorites", the model's word for films rated above your own average, and count rated films only, as home does. If the unrated watches should show too (the read counts them), add a second figure later, "plus 23 watched without rating", not a merged one.
  • Visitors: the same line in their voice ("104 of their favorites") or hide counts on others' profiles; I'd show them, as the films are already public on the profile.
  • Stand-ins: the counts (104 / 49 / 73, sized to the bar) and the third film in each row (Zodiac, Before Sunrise, Dune: Part Two, all rated 4.5 by @alexfiacco). Real: the bar, the vs lines, the first two films per row (whySpecific), the 474.
