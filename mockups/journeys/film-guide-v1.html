Why the gaps are what they are: 14,513 rows keep only a "slim" raw_tmdb (no companies, budget or revenue), and 47,850 have no raw_tmdb at all, so the full /movie record was never kept for them. Of the well-known films that do have the full record, 99.9% have a studio, 73.9% a budget and 79.5% a box office figure: that is about the ceiling after a backfill (TMDB leaves budget at 0 for many films). Labels cover 27,800 films, nearly all the well-known ones; the long tail gets them as the label backlog runs.

What needs fetching, and the recommendation

1. One TMDB pass, free. GET /movie/{id}?append_to_response=release_dates for every film missing the full record: about 62,000 films (55,000 if we stop at films with any Letterboxd watches). One call gives studio, budget, revenue, release date, original title, spoken languages and every country's age rating. lib/certification.mjs already makes this exact call and throws away all but one country; keep the whole release_dates list in a new film_certifications table (slug, country, rating, type). TMDB's API is free; at a polite 20 calls a second it is under an hour, at 5 a second about 3.5 hours. Then the daily label-films cron keeps new films current. The well-known 9,971 first: about 5,200 calls, under 5 minutes.

2. Two new label traits, language (swearing) and substances (0 to 100, like violence), added to lib/label-traits.ts and run over the 27,800 labelled films by the existing label-traits job. This is the only part that costs money (model calls, the same per-film cost as the last trait pass). Until it runs, the Language and Substances rows are hidden, and Substances can show "Some" from the drug use warning.

3. No rating reason from anywhere free. TMDB does not carry the MPA's "for language, some sexual content" text and we will not take IMDb's parents guide (its terms forbid it). "Mostly for violence and language" is ours, built from the two highest levels, and only when a level is Moderate or above.

Recommendation: A, one row after Where to watch. It answers "can I put this on with the kids" in the closed state (badge, region, the two strongest levels) and costs about 92pt, against nearly 500pt for B on every film. The avoid flag still shows closed, so nothing a viewer has asked about is hidden. It sits after Where to watch because both are "should we watch this tonight" facts, and before Discussion, which is about the film once seen. Details moves into the same row, so the tabs drop to Themes · Cast · Crew and there is one home for facts. Build order: the row with today's data (rating, three levels, warnings, runtime, language, country, release date, collection), then the TMDB pass fills Studio, Budget, Box office and the viewer's own region, then the two new traits fill the last two rows.
