Are we overusing it? Every place it would sit

✓Already ticked (rules 1253/1254): every text drawer’s heading (Film DNA strand, Our read, Where to watch, Filters, share drawers, the other content drawers), “Rate films you love”.
+Old dash → tick: Settings, ☰ Menu, About / Privacy / Terms / Help pages, a Browse door page (Mood, Genre, Collection, World shelf) and a Lane / Theme page title.
+New: Pitch (moved to the heading block), film-led drawers, Moods / Genres / Decades / Collections drawers, Chats, The 15 Archetypes (page and drawer).
−Old dash removed, no tick: “Lanes you share” / “You both love” (section labels in a friend preview), the list view’s film count, a movie night room’s kicker. These are not titles.
✕Never: section heads inside a screen (Previous takes, From the writers’ room, Settings groups, World cinema, Receipts, Defining films), cards, rows, empty states, buttons, toasts, the tab roots (Picks, Browse, Social, Profile: the tab bar says where you are and they carry the hero’s oxblood or the ticket red), the film page, the reveal.
  • Count: about 25 screens and drawers, each with exactly one tick, always in the same spot. That reads as a signature, not decoration. It turns into overuse only if it reaches section heads, which this keeps out.
  • One red per screen still holds. The tick is the screen’s red. So a film-led drawer should not keep the oxblood poster offset as well, which is why B is the pick.

Decide

  1. Tick under every page and drawer title, never section heads Rec
    Settings, Menu, Pitch, Chats, Archetypes, the Browse drawers and pages, the info pages. The old oxblood dash is retired everywhere.
  2. Film-led drawers: tick under “year · director”, drop the poster offset (B) Rec
    Or keep the subtle offset as well (A): two reds side by side.
  3. Moods / Genres / Decades drawers: add a short kicker (“12 moods · ranked for you”) so the tick sits under a two-line heading like every other drawer. Or the title alone with the tick.
  4. Tab roots stay unticked. Say if you want them too (Picks, Browse, Social, Profile). That is the line where it would start to feel overused.
