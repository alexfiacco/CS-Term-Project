Where the red dash sits

Every drawer drawn at 390pt from origin/main: the dash is RedDash (rule 1107, 18 × 3, oxblood #7A1220, capsule; the web's .rd), the kicker is DrawerKicker / DrawerHeader (rule 1121). Each drawer is shown four ways: A today, the dash 8 before the text; B the dash 8 after the text on its line, centred on the x-height; C1 a short underline (the dash's own 18) under the first letters, 3 below the text; C2 the underline the width of the label. One red per screen throughout; the film page's section labels carry their own blue dash (14 × 2, rule 673), shown last.

A · Today

HStack(spacing: 8) { RedDash(); Text }. Centred on the line box. Takes no height, adds 26 to the line's width.

B · After

Text, 8, RedDash, on the text's baseline line, centred on the x-height. Takes no height. The kicker's ellipsis has to stop before it.

C1 · Under, short pick

VStack(alignment: .leading, spacing: 3) { Text; RedDash() } around the label only (the part before the first "·"). Adds 6 to the heading's height.

C2 · Under, word

Same, but the dash runs the label's width ("Discussion", "Our read"). Tested and dropped: at full width it is a link's or a selected tab's underline.

7Where each one breaksthe same marks, the awkward headings

A · Today, the dash before

Works everywhere. No extra height, never confused with a link, survives wrapping and truncation, reads the same on caps and sentence kickers. The cost the owner is reacting to: it pushes every heading 26 to the right of the gutter, and on a centred heading it pulls the text off-centre.

B · After the text

Breaks most often. It lands after whatever ends the line, so on "Discussion · 2 friends · 14 takes" it marks the count, not the drawer; on a truncated kicker it sits after the ellipsis; on a two-line heading it dangles at the end of the second line. Reads as a trailing dash in the sentence. Fine only on a one-word label ("Our read", "Pitch").

C1 · Short underline recommended

Best of the two new ones. The heading starts on the gutter again, the dash anchors the label (never the count after "·"), and it centres cleanly under a centred heading. At 18 long it reads as a mark, not as an underline, so it does not look like a link.

Rules it needs: under the label only, never the "·" part; under the last line of a heading that wraps; 8 extra above a tab row (Discussion), whose blue 2pt rule is the one other underline nearby. Keep the film page's blue section dash on the left: C on every section label stacks a column of underlines down the page next to "See all".
