Profile: a headline and a picture first, the paragraphs one tap down

Today the Taste tab opens on four kinds of text before the first poster: an eyebrow, a streak sentence with three links, a "How you got your mix" row, a paragraph with Read more, then three figures each with two sub-lines and a crowd line under them. Nothing is wrong in any one of them; together they read as homework. The proposal keeps every fact and puts one line plus one visual on screen per idea, with one clear button into the detail.

Taller backdrop up behind the status bar. Name, one line, a bigger labelled mix bar, one "Why Nihilist" button. Figures without sub-lines. Bigger posters.

One drawer holds what Read more and How you got your mix held: the mix in words, each type's films, your full read, all 17 archetypes.

What changed and why

  • Backdrop: 216 to 300 tall and up behind the status bar, so the profile opens on a picture. The @handle leaves the bar (it is in Edit profile and on Share); the bell and menu stay.
  • Photo and name: 64 to 80, Bodoni 20 to 28. The bio is held to one line here; a tap shows the rest.
  • Archetype: the streak sentence ("Streaks of Tenderhearted and IMAX Idealist") becomes the names printed under their own segments of a 10 tall bar. The archetype's one-line tag replaces the first sentence of the read. Read more and How you got your mix collapse into one button, Why Nihilist, so there is one obvious next step.
  • Figures: the six sub-lines and the crowd line go into each figure's tap (the same drawers they open today). The numbers get bigger: 22 to 30.
  • Films that define you: posters 128 to 156 wide. The rank numerals were Bodoni, which the house rules keep for match % and Our read only, so they go; the order still says rank.

Honest notes

  • The ticket and chips stay as approved on 2026-11-07 (v19). They are already short. The one red on the screen is the ticket's divider, so the archetype's red spine and the oxblood lead segment become cream; that is a change to rule 1303's look and worth saying out loud.
  • Hiding the read behind a tap costs something: the paragraph is the most personal thing Montage writes, and a few people will never open it. The tag line is generic (every Nihilist has it). If that feels too thin, keep the read's first sentence instead of the tag and still drop everything else; the word count stays under half of today's.
  • B looks better in a still and worse in use: it competes with the backdrop for "the picture", and the same films then appear again under Films that define. I would ship A.

Simplify · 2 of 3 · choosing your backdrop

Backdrop: your choice, in Edit profile, with a preview

How it works now (lib/profile-backdrop.ts): the still of your first Canon film that has one; with no Canon, or a hidden one, your highest-rated film's still. Nothing on screen says so, there is no control, and switching "Show my Canon" off quietly swaps the picture. The proposal adds a Backdrop control to Edit profile and makes the backdrop's credit the shortcut on your own profile. Automatic stays the default, so nobody who never touches it sees a change.

Now · Edit profile
Photo, Username, Name, Bio, two switches. No backdrop anywhere; it follows Canon #1.
Proposed · Edit profile opens on your header
The header as others see it, with Change backdrop on the still and the pencil on the photo. One line says where the picture comes from.
Proposed · Choose a backdrop
A live preview on top in the house grade, then your Canon or your top-rated films as stills. Automatic is the first tile.
Proposed · back on your profile
The new backdrop. On your own profile the credit carries a pencil and is a 44 button into the same picker (option B).

Options and recommendation

  • A: Edit profile only. One place, impossible to tap by accident, matches where the photo is changed.
  • B: A, plus the credit on your own backdrop is a button (pencil beside the film's name, same picker). Someone who wonders "how do I change this" is looking at the picture when they wonder it.
  • I recommend B, but only the credit, not the whole backdrop: a full-width invisible tap target on the top third of the profile would fire when people mean to scroll or pull to refresh, and it does nothing on anyone else's profile, which would be inconsistent.
  • Where you think you are wrong-footed: tying it to Canon #1 is a fine default and worth keeping as Automatic. The confusing part is that it is invisible and that the Canon switch silently changes it. With a chosen film, hiding your Canon no longer moves the picture.

DB: one nullable column (not run)

-- Profile backdrop choice (simplify-profile-v1 sheet 2).
-- Null = Automatic (today's rule: Canon #1's still,
-- else the highest-rated film's). Safe on the live DB:
-- one nullable column, no rewrite, nothing changed.
alter table public.public_profiles
  add column if not exists backdrop_slug text;
comment on column public.public_profiles.backdrop_slug is
  'Letterboxd slug of the film whose still is the profile backdrop; null = automatic.';
  • No foreign key: a film row can be missing or lose its still; the reader falls back to Automatic, as it does today when Canon #1 has no still.
  • One still per film (films.backdrop_url). Choosing among a film's several stills would need the TMDB images endpoint and a URL column; not in v1.

  • New GET /api/profile-backdrops: your Canon and top-rated films that have stills (the picker's tiles), from lib/user-canon.ts and the public library.
  • Web: ProfileMenu.tsx (the header preview and the picker), Result.tsx (credit as a button when it's yours). iPhone: AccountScreens.swift EditProfileView, ProfileBackdrop.swift.
  • Follows for free: the list page header (rule 1364), the profile skeleton and the share card read the same backdrop.

Blend: the number, what to watch, then one line per idea

The Blend page today is eleven stacked sections, and the first thing you can act on (films on both your watchlists) is the eighth. The proposal answers three questions in order: how close are we, what should we watch, why. Every section that is a paragraph or a list becomes a one-line row with its preview; the row opens the same content in a drawer.

What changed and why

  • The hero grows (faces 56 to 72, the % 64 to 80) and the band loses its italic (house rule: no italics).
  • Watch together moves from eighth to first after Invite: it is the one section that answers "what do I do here". Posters 140 wide, the % for you both under each, ratings each way on the film.
  • You both love keeps its posters and drops the two face-and-rating pairs under each; they are on the film sheet.
  • Film DNA: the takeaway becomes the heading ("You both lean Intensity"), the three shared strands are named, the other six only drawn; a tap on the shape opens the strand drawer as today.
  • Four rows replace Lanes you share, both Picks folds, How your ratings line up, Where you differ and Most surprising agreement. Each shows a short preview and opens a drawer.

Honest notes

  • Where you differ was open by default on purpose (rule 425): people liked reading it. As a row it gets fewer reads. Its preview ("Slow burn vs Spectacle") is the hook; if it underperforms, open it again, it is one flag.
  • Picks each way are only for mutual follows; when they are not there, the row is not drawn (not a row that says "follow each other").
  • The joined state (blend-page, approved 2026-11-03) keeps its list on top; this only reorders what sits under it, the same way.
