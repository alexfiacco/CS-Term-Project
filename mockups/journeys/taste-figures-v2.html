Recommendation: D

  • D keeps the row exactly as picked (3.6★ / Your average / 1,260 films, 2,446 / Hours watched / about 102 days, 3% / Against the crowd / 36 films) and gives the crowd its own sentence, where it can say what it is: the crowd on the same films, not a site-wide average. 230 wide, one line at every phone width.
  • Wording. "The crowd gives the same films 3.7★", UI 13 faint, the figure in muted. It says the fact and lets the reader compare with 3.6 just above it. "Crowd average 3.7 · you rate 0.1 lower" (also measured, fits) spells out a difference of a tenth, which reads as more than it is. With a gap of 0.5 or more the line could add it ("you rate 0.6 higher"); not needed at 0.1.
  • Space: the row is unchanged; the section grows by 24 (8 + one 16 line). Then 16 to the next section label as now.
  • Why not the others. A wraps at every width and puts a rating under a percentage. B leaves 5 to spare at 320 and doubles the numbers in one column. C hides it behind a tap nothing advertises.
  • When it shows: only with all three figures (the average's small line is the count then). With two figures the average keeps "from 1,260 ratings · crowd 3.7" as today, so the line under is not needed. No crowd average known: no line.
