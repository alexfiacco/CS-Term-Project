Wording: say what a word means once, and what a button will do

Three passes over the copy. Montage’s own words get one plain line the first time they come up, and then they are left alone. Every empty screen gets one sentence and one button that moves you forward. Buttons say what happens next. Quotes are from montage main as read on 2026-11-07; web and iPhone share the copy unless a line says otherwise.

1First-time lines for Montage’s words

Each line shows once, under the word, the first time that word is on screen for you, and is gone the next time. It is the app’s existing term tip (TermTip.tsx TERMS; iPhone AccountHints, the same montage_hint_seen:term-<id> keys on the account), with one change: it marks itself seen when shown, so there is no ✕ to tap. The (i) the web keeps after the tip stays as the way back. Lane and Taste Mixes keep the tips they have; Canon’s is tightened, and match % gets one on the iPhone, which has none today.

  • Under the word, not in a box. Newsreader 15 on 20, muted, 8 under the word’s line. No panel, no icon, no close: it reads as part of the page and is simply not there next time.
  • One line per screen at a time. If two new words land on one screen (the reveal has archetype, streak and Film DNA), show the first one only; the others get theirs the next time they are on screen. Three tips at once read as a manual.
  • The word starts the sentence, so a line still makes sense if someone skims it: “Your archetype is…”, “A streak is…”.
  • Voice: second person, present tense, no “simply”, “just” or “magic”; the numbers only where they help (match %).

2Empty states

One recipe (rule 638): the screen’s art, one sentence that says what will be here and how it gets here, and one button that does that. Titles like “Lately is quiet” and “Quiet in here” go: they are mood, not information, and the sentence under them already says it. A quiet text link may stay where it is a real second way in (Invite by link).

3Buttons that should say what happens next

A label is the next thing that happens, in the fewest plain words. Words that collide with a tab or a feature (“picks”, “list”, “cut”) are avoided in labels that mean something else.
