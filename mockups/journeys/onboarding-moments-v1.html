Onboarding moments, page 1 of 2: the journey as it is

The owner asked: "On onboarding journey do we have enough moments to keep the user excited about the app?" This page maps the way in as it runs on origin/main today (iPhone Features/Onboarding and the web's TasteTest, LovedStart, LockedIdentity, WelcomeTour), step by step, with what each step asks and what it gives back. Page 2 ranks seven new moments and mocks the top four at 390pt. Review and mocks only, no app code changed.

VerdictNot yet. One great peak, then a quiet ending.

The reveal is a real moment: the name develops like a print, the why line counts their own films, Film DNA draws, and three picks fan in with a match on each. The 15 ready card in the deck is a good second beat, and an invited visit ends on "You and Alex: 78%". But the first two to three minutes give back nothing about the person (by design since rule 853), the peak is followed by about 90 seconds of chores (save, handle, a six card tour that re-shows the picks), the Letterboxd wait shows a counter rather than their own films, and the first session ends with no reason to open the app tomorrow: no plan for tonight, no reminder tied to a film, no friend on the iPhone reveal, and the streak and goal never come up. The fixes are small; most reuse parts that already exist.

Assessment

Where the aha moments land

  • The reveal at about 3.5 minutes is the one big peak, and it is well made: develop, why line from their own counts, "Streak · Nihilist", "11 deciding films settled it", Film DNA, a fan of picks with a %.
  • The 15 ready card names the archetype early and offers a choice. A real second beat.
  • First match % lands only in the reveal's fan and the tour's card 1. It never appears on a film they know.
  • Invited visits get a second peak, "You and Alex: 78%". Everyone else gets no friend moment on iPhone.
  • Film DNA appears once, dashed, "Early read". The firm-up at 30 happens later, alone, as a toast.

Long stretches, few payoffs

  • Ratings 1 to 14. About 20 taps over two minutes with only a count and a grey ring. Rule 853 keeps taste out of the deck before 15, so this is a choice, but it is the longest dry run in the flow, right at the start.
  • Save, handle, tour. Right after the peak, three steps and about 90 seconds before the app is usable. The tour's card 1 shows the pick they saw seconds ago.
  • The Letterboxd wait. Up to three minutes on "1,204 of 1,640". The richest data we will ever get from them, and none of it is on screen.
  • The Letterboxd export itself is effort outside our control. The peek's "Importing as @name" is the only feedback.

Drop-off risks

  • Deck 5 to 12: the point where the count still looks far ("6 of 30" in the header, the ring under half) and nothing has been given.
  • The save drawer: the first account ask, placed at the peak. Fine, but the locked third pick is the only reason given.
  • The import wait: people leave a progress screen that is not about them. The 45s stall line helps only when it stalls.
  • Day two: the largest risk. Nothing in session one asks them to come back.

The hook to come back

  • Tonight's pick: shown three times (reveal, tour, Three for tonight), never committed to. The "Did you watch" local notification exists (Watching this tonight, next morning 10:00) but onboarding never offers it.
  • Notifications: asked on the tour's last card, about nothing in particular.
  • Streak: the weekly strip lives on Profile; its reminder is off until switched on. Never mentioned in session one.
  • Goal: offered once in January only (rule 1105).
  • A friend: only the invited get one. On the web, the reveal's friend line is the only seed.

How strong taste apps do it

  • Wrapped-style reveals break one reveal into many small facts, each its own beat, each about you. Our import reads a whole history and shows a counter.
  • Letterboxd's hook is the diary: you come back to log what you watched. Our next-morning "How was it?" is the same loop, already built, unused here.
  • Beli answers every ranking at once with where it landed, so each tap pays. Our deck answers nothing until 15, then predicts nothing it could check.
  • Duolingo lands a win in the first minute, then sets day one of a streak and a reminder time before you leave.

What is already right

  • Thresholds are honest (rules 202, 239): no % or name before 15, and the copy never invents.
  • The reveal's craft: the develop, the oxblood rule, the why line from real counts.
  • The deck never loses a run: every tap is saved, a call or a closed app costs nothing.
  • Three for tonight ends on films, not settings. The proposals below build on it rather than adding screens.
