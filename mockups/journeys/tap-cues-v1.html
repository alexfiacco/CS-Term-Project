Tap cues: one look for “this opens something”

Today the app has three ways of saying a thing can be tapped (a ›, a solid underline, a dotted underline) and many things that open something say nothing at all: the Against the crowd figure, a director’s name, the ticket’s counts, the red avoid line. A few things that do nothing look like controls: a theme chip with no page, the blue popularity word, the outlined Early read pill. Proposed: one cue per kind of element, and no cue means nothing happens.

A row or a line that opens something ends in ›Lucide chevron-right in --faint, 12 to 16 by the line’s size. Rows, cards, figures, the avoid line, chips (the pill plus ›).
A word inside a sentence that opens something has a dotted underlineLovestruck in ink, the underline at 45% of the ink, 3 below the baseline. Streak names, a director’s name. Never a solid underline (it reads as emphasis).
Posters and faces carry no cueEveryone taps a poster. The match stamp on a poster stays as it is and needs no mark of its own.
No cue, no tapAnything that does nothing loses what makes it look like a control: no pill outline, no link blue.

Notes

  • The poster stamp is the one exception worth deciding. A tap on a poster opens the film; a tap on its % stamp opens the Why drawer (rule 708). Under the rule the stamp carries no cue because it is part of a poster. That is fine as long as the Why drawer is also one tap away on the film page (Our read ›), which it is. Do not add a › or an underline to a 14pt stamp.
  • The ticket’s counts are the one place a social-app habit (tap a follower count) meets the rule. Adding › makes the four cells one family with the blend. Its cost, measured in the frame: at 390 with a four-digit film count the stub falls back to its short form, “blend ›”, through the ViewThatFits the ticket already has (ProfileBackdrop.swift:172).   • Film DNA’s “Tap a strand to read it” (ProfileSections.swift:269) can go once each strand label carries its ›: the cue does the hint’s job.
  • Nothing here changes size, spacing or colour beyond the cue itself. The › is always --faint, the dotted underline always 45% of the word’s own colour, so neither adds a new colour.
