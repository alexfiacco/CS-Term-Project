1 · Scout + Viewfinder MY PICK

"Scout me a film…". Directors scout locations through a viewfinder: you describe the shot, we go and find it. A verb on the button, a noun inside ("Your scout", "2 of 3 scouts this month").

2 · Spotlight + Spotlight

"Spotlight: something tense and short". The catalogue goes dark and a light finds the films that fit. Warm and visual; a little long on small phones and the web uses "spotlight" in archetype copy.

3 · Dream up + Wand

"Dream up a film…". The most inviting; says you can ask for anything ("Alien but funny"). Two words and a touch whimsical next to the serif calm.

Open for you

The mark, pick one of 12: 1 Viewfinder, 2 Projector beam, 3 Megaphone, 4 Ticket spark, 5 Wand over film, 6 Lens glint, 7 Slate chat, 8 Popcorn spark, 9 Reel to words, 10 Spotlight, 11 Frame cursor, 12 Scout lens. The name, pick one of 16: Scout, Spotlight, Dream up, Reel me, Find me, Direct, Frame, Sketch, Screen, Cast, Vibe, Prompt, Wishlist, Roll, Cut to, Tonight. My pick: Scout with the Viewfinder (1). Five letters, fits the button on every phone, a real film-making word that still reads plainly to everyone ("scout" = go and find), works as a verb and a noun, and the viewfinder is literally the tool a director scouts with, so mark and word explain each other. The viewfinder also holds up best at 20pt (four corners and a dot). Runner-up: Spotlight with its own mark, if you want warmth over brevity. Avoid: Tonight (already the Tonight tab), Cast and Screen (clash with the Cast tab and "screen" as a verb on TV), Prompt (sounds like an AI tool), Wishlist (sounds like the watchlist). Typing a sentence in search still opens the same sheet, as today.
