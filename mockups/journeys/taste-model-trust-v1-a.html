03Always say why

One reason line, one pattern, everywhere a film is put in front of you: a grey lead-in, then the evidence in blue, and the evidence is always yours (your films, your strands, your people). Bodoni roman 15, never italic. First that applies: 1 "Because you loved A & B" (the kNN anchors, from 70%, as Picks does today) · 2 "Leans into your slow-burn immersion" (the film's strongest strand among your top three) · 3 "Sam gave it 5★" (people you follow) · 4 "Close to the films you rate 4★ and up". Never "Recommended", "Popular", "Trending" or "for you" alone.

05Honest disclosure

The suggested line, refined against the code: AI does more than read Pitch (it writes Our read and some pick lines, and labels films once for everyone), and it never ranks or scores. Saying all three is what makes "your model ranks the picks" believable.

Your data, in plain English
How your picks are made

Montage builds a taste model from your ratings: your Film DNA, your archetype and a match % for every film. That model, which is Montage's own code, does all the ranking and sets every %. AI (Anthropic's Claude) helps with words only. It reads what you type into Pitch and turns it into filters you can edit, it writes some sentences such as Our read on a film page, and it describes each film once, the same for everyone. It never chooses or scores a film for you, and it is set not to train on your data.

You can see the model at work: every pick says why, and your profile shows how often its guesses matched your stars.

Why "78%, where your 4★s sit" and not "4★"

Turning the % into stars would invent a second prediction the model does not make. Pairing the % with your own star's typical % is the exact Called it test, says it in one breath, and teaches what the % means on day one.

··Recommendations
  1. Fix the low lean before the score goes public. 104 of 137 live misses are "You liked it more", and in-app ratings miss that way 51 of 67 times. Today's posthoc correction is off by default; turning on (or retuning) that calibration for shown guesses would lift the live Called it rate more than any copy can. Ship the record after it.
  2. Count only shown guesses in the record; use held-out guesses only on day one. Shown rows are the honest record (a trigger already stops held-out rows replacing them). Held-out rows are 62% called and great for the reveal, but mixing them in would inflate the profile number.
  3. Record "shown" honestly. match_predictions captures the stored match as shown even where the screen showed no % (the Discover deck rates with source: "grid", 116 of 196 rows). Add a displayed flag (the client's predicted, as guess-check uses) and count only displayed guesses.
  4. Fill the learning groundwork columns. model_version and raw_predicted are set on 2 of 196 rated rows and holdout on none, so "your read got sharper" cannot yet be shown as "the record rose after the model learned". One week of logging makes the trend line real.
  5. Store the verdict. verdictFor runs against expectedByStar, which moves as the model re-scores; the same guess can flip verdict later. Freeze the verdict (and the star table value) at rating time so a past "Called it" never quietly becomes a miss.
  6. Window: "of your last 20" until most people have 40. Only 3 people have 40+ shown guesses. 20 with a 10-guess floor reads the same and appears for more people.
  7. One voice for learning. Merge rule 489's "Your picks just got sharper" into "Your read got sharper", always with the strand that moved (3+ points) or the plain line, never an invented change.
  8. Never let AI write the reason line. The reason line is the model's evidence; AI-written lines sit under it. That is what makes the disclosure ("AI helps with words only") true on every screen.
  9. The day-one three are chosen before guessing (one per star band, informative films first) and shown whatever happened, with the 2 of 3 copy ready. Cherry-picking hits would be the one thing here that is not honest.
