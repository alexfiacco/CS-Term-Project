Montage's read: the share card, three ways

The owner on the iPhone card: "lots of text here, and also the black background. I don't know, it's just very dark." Today the card is drawn on the server (POST /api/read-card, lib/read-card.tsx readCardBody, 1080 x 1920 PNG, the kit in lib/card-kit.tsx) and the iPhone (FilmScreen.swift ReadShareSheet) and web (Result.tsx ShareReadSheet) only show that PNG, so one change fixes both. The read is the film's "Our read" (res.explanation / model.explanation, lib/taste/match.ts: two sentences, about 40 words, never more than 48; the route accepts 20 to 700 characters) and goes onto the card whole, at 44 down to 30px.

The "you" problem. The read's second sentence is written to the reader ("You enjoy films that let talk do the heavy lifting"). On a card the sharer posts as "4★ from me", that "you" now points at the friend looking at it. All three options print only the film sentence (the read's first sentence, which the prompt already makes the film's craft or feel, never the viewer), capped at about 140 to 160 characters; Aftersun on C shows the two-sentence path, which needs the second sentence written in the first person, not "you".

Recommendation: A

A answers both complaints and changes the least. One sentence instead of two cuts the card from 61 words to about 27, and the read grows from 44 to 54px, so it reads at the size an Instagram story or a Messages bubble shows it. The film's own colour replaces the flat black: on Instagram's black story frame and in a dark Messages thread a #0b0b0d card disappears into the chrome, while a blurred still makes every read look like its film. It stays inside the share-card kit (rule 1109: dark ground, Bodoni roman, blue number and pill, spine as the one red), so it still sits with the other cards. The blur also hides a soft w500 poster, which B cannot.

B is the boldest and the most "film", but needs a larger poster or a backdrop for every title, and a poster's own lettering gets cut. C is the most different and pops in a dark feed, but it would be the only light card in a set that is dark by rule, and it needs a second blue; worth keeping as an idea for a printed or "ticket" moment, not for this card alone.

The "you" fix, whichever option: the card prints the read's first sentence only (the film half; split on the first sentence end). If that sentence still has "you" or "your", or runs past about 160 characters, the card drops the read line and keeps title, stars and foot. The app's Our read keeps both sentences. A two-sentence card (C's Aftersun) would need a separate first-person line from the model.
